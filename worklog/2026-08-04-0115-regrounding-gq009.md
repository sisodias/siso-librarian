## The grounding actually dereferences

Every citation carries a byte range, and I checked all three resolve to their exact quoted text rather than trusting the offsets I extracted:

- `sources/internet-archive/adapter-contract.json` [1086:1120] → `"required_rights": "public domain"`
- `metrics/2026-08-03-ia-live-metadata-probe-combined.json` [195:219] → `"numFound_live": 1367676`
- `worklog/2026-08-04-0100-refresh-ledger-earns-its-claim.md` [376:400] → `a hand-written assertion`

Then I made that check permanent instead of a thing I did once by hand. The verifier now dereferences every in-repo grounding range and fails if it no longer matches. External sources — standing packets, upstream corpora — are skipped, since they are not resolvable from here.

This closes a real silent-failure mode. A claim whose evidence has shifted underneath it still validates structurally: schema fine, portfolio fine, ledger fine, and the quote pointing at whatever now occupies those bytes. That is the plausible-wrong-answer failure, and it is exactly the kind that survives review.

Verified in both directions rather than assumed: corrupting one range to `start: 999` makes `npm run verify` exit 1 with `byte_range [999:1120] ... no longer matches the quote`; restoring it exits 0. The corrupted copy was written to `/tmp` and the real claim restored and re-verified afterwards.

## A schema decision I made rather than worked around

The ledger enum was `fresh | stale | blocked`, and a superseded claim is none of those. The tempting move was to call it `stale` — technically survivable, quietly wrong, since stale means *needs regrounding* while superseded means *a successor already exists*. Conflating them would make the ledger lie about what work remains.

So I widened the enum to include `superseded` in both the verifier and the evaluator, where it counts as terminal: a retired claim cannot drift, so it never triggers a disagreement.

The verifier caught my first attempt (`superseded_by_v2`, not a valid value) before it reached a commit. That is the gate doing its job on me, which is the point of having it.

## Residual

Two claims and one question is a thin portfolio. The contract now has a successor, verified grounding, derived freshness, and a rot detector — but the strongest test is a second question with genuinely different shape, and that does not exist yet. Confidence at 0.81 reflects that, and I would rather it read 0.81 with a reason than 0.95 with a story.
