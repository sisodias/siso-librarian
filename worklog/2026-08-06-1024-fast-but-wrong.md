# Worklog: search was fast and returned the wrong passages

I spent a long stretch making search **fast** — 434 seconds down to 551ms — and
never once checked whether its answers were **right**.

```
$ grep -n 'rank\|bm25\|order by' scripts/search-library.mjs
(nothing)
```

**No ordering at all.** A reader got whatever rows FTS5 yielded first: rowid
order, which is the alphabetically-first book by identifier.

## What that cost

Mean occurrences of the search term across the top 5 results:

| term | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| quinine | 1.0 | **5.2** |
| telescope | 1.2 | **3.6** |
| botany | 1.2 | **4.0** |
| vaccination | 1.4 | **3.8** |

**Every unranked top hit mentioned the term exactly once.** For "quinine" the
best passage in the corpus mentions it **nine times** and no reader ever saw it.
The new top hit is a London hospital pharmacopoeia:

```
Haustus [Quinine] vel [Quinine] Sulphatis. [Quinine] Sulphate 1 gr.…
```

## Three structural lessons, each measured

**A rowid restriction does not bound ranking.** With the cap outside the
subquery, "such" took **208 seconds** — `ORDER BY bm25` still scored every match.
Bounding the MATCH *inside* the subquery costs 62ms and returns the **identical**
top 5 as full ranking.

**`snippet()` and `bm25()` both work inside a capped subquery.** I had assumed
`snippet()` forced the MATCH to stay in the outer query — that assumption is what
blocked the obvious fix for two attempts.

**Join order dominated everything.** Joining `book_ext` before the exclusion made
"himself" take **87 seconds**. Cap+rank alone is 65ms; the exclusion adds 68ms.
Resolving a title for all 40,000 candidates before discarding nearly all of them
was the entire cost. Exclude, rank, limit, **then** join: **798ms**.
