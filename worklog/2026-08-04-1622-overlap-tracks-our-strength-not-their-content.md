# Worklog: overlap tracks our strength, not their content

Date: 2026-08-04T16:22:45Z (generated filename)
Thread: was 97.5% a science-fiction artifact?

## Why I did not stop at one number

The overlap sample came from **science fiction — the Library's strongest
subject at 3,291 books**. That is the worst case for finding new material, so
97.5% could not settle the general question.

I had just retracted a proposal for reporting a funnel's output as its yield.
Reporting one subject's overlap as the corpus rate would be the same error.

## Four subjects

```
subject                        library   sample  held   overlap
Science fiction                  3,291       40    39    97.5%
Detective and mystery stories    1,013       30    29    96.7%
Historical fiction               1,080       30    28    93.3%
Cookery                            384       20    13    65.0%
```

**Overlap tracks library strength, not IA content.**

Where this Library holds thousands, IA offers almost nothing new. Where it holds
hundreds, a third of the sample is new. Both corpora draw heavily on Gutenberg,
so they converge exactly where Gutenberg is deep.

## What that does to my own proposal

I aimed the subject filter at the Library's **top** subjects — precisely where
it yields least. The inversion is obvious in hindsight: to find new books, filter
for what the Library is *weak* in.

But that does not rescue the numbers. IA has **60 cookery texts total**. The
pools where novelty is high are small; the pools that are large are already held.

## The ceiling

Nothing measured today suggests IA expansion adds thousands of books to this
Library. It suggests **tens to low hundreds**.

That may still be worth having — a hundred books the Library lacks is not
nothing. But it is a different decision from the one I put to Shaan this
morning, and he should be choosing on the real magnitude.
